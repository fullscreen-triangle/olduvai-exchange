# 36 — Items as a causal knowledge graph, without a reasoner, with split agents

Status: **design**. Nothing here is implemented.

⚠️ **This note is not note 32/34/35's shape.** Those extracted from code the author explicitly
disowned. The three sources here are the opposite: papers by the same author as this repo, and
the extraction is *from established results into an existing design*, not from a disowned
prototype. There is no "I am not endorsing this" clause to honour, and no disclaimer to inherit.

The three sources:

| | |
|---|---|
| `levinthal/models/publications/causal-knowledge-graphs/causal-knowledge-graphs.tex` | *On Causality as a Role in Protein Knowledge Graphs: Addressing Without Storage, Edges Without Curation* |

Three separable claims, and this note takes them in that order.

## 1. ⭐ The graph already exists here, and it is already the causal kind

`/dashboard/graph` in `lib/navigation.js` carries the blurb **"The occupied trie this is all
addressed in"**. That sentence was written as a description of a view. It is in fact the
architecture, stated correctly and by accident.

`causal-knowledge-graphs.tex` §11 (`cor:no-edges`) is the theorem:

> the construction *"stores neither nodes nor edges... edges between u and v are generated by
> evaluating [trie-factoring] at ℓ(u,v), **which requires only the two addresses and hence no
> adjacency structure**."*

⭐ **This is exactly what `crates/olduvai-core/src/trie.rs` does and does not do.** Ranking is
by longest common prefix. There is no edge table. There never was one, and the reason there
never was one was the AI exclusion on ranking — *"no parameters"* — rather than a graph-theoretic
argument. The paper supplies the argument retroactively: a weight of the form
`w = f(longest common prefix)` (`def:trie-factoring`) is not an optimisation, it is the thing
that makes an adjacency structure unnecessary.

So the honest statement of the current position is:

| Claim | Status here |
|---|---|
| Items are arranged as a graph | ✅ already — the trie |
| Nodes are stored | ❌ and correctly so — an address is computed, `address.rs` |
| Edges are stored | ❌ and correctly so — there is no edge table anywhere in `crates/` |
| The graph is *causal* rather than associative | ⚠️ **this is the part that is not yet true** |

### 1.1 ⚠️ What "causal" would actually require, and it is not vocabulary

The temptation is to read "causal knowledge graph" as "knowledge graph with causal edge labels".
That is the thing the paper is arguing against. `cor:crossref` in the same paper:

> *"the rot is not a failure of engineering discipline but a consequence of identity having been
> located in the label"*

A `causes` edge label is a label. It carries no invariant. `navigation.js` already refuses the
equivalent move on the observation side — the satellites entry has `constrains: "within"` with a
comment explaining that `overpass` is not a variant `olduvai-core` has, *"and the server would
refuse it with `wrong_shape`"*. That is the same discipline: the name names a thing that exists
in the type, or it is not written.

⭐ **What makes the paper's graph causal is `cor:no-return` — the point at which a static graph
acquires time.** Separation costs are asymmetric under propagation, so "u reaches v" and "v
reaches u" are different questions with different answers, and the asymmetry is *derived from the
weighting*, not asserted by an edge direction. For items here that would mean: a consignment's
relation to a holding is not a stored `harvested_from`; it is a separation cost computed from two
addresses, and it is asymmetric because separating a consignment from its origin and separating
an origin from one of its consignments are not the same cut.

⚠️ **That is an open claim, not a result.** The paper derives it from a physical contact graph
where `w({u,v}) = Λ/‖ς(u)−ς(v)‖` (`thm:weighting-derived`) with Λ cancelling from every ratio.
Nothing here has established the analogue of ς for a consignment. §5 below says what would.

## 2. ⭐⭐ Why the proteomics route is *replaced* rather than copied

The described method is: ontology → construct KG → run reasoner → generate KG. The paper's §13
(`sec:queries`) is a direct answer to why that route is not adopted here, and the answer is
stronger than a preference.

**`thm:query-separation` (Retrieval cannot express admissibility).** Two graphs with *identical
edge sets* — V = {v₀, x\*, y₁…y_n, med}, edges {v₀,x\*} plus {u,med} for every u. Identical under
every retrieval query, because retrieval is pattern matching and path composition over stored
triples. Their weights differ only on the medium edges at the y_i (1 vs 2). The stretch
str(v₀,x\*) = 2 in both, but the floor flo₁ = 1 and flo₂ = 2, so the propagation is 0-accountable
in one and not in the other.

⭐ `rem:separation-meaning` names the mechanism:

> *"**accountability is a global property expressed in local terms**, and a stored graph records
> only the local terms... One may store weights as triple attributes and still not recover flo:
> it is a minimum over items of [sepcost], which is itself a minimum over cuts, and **neither
> minimisation is a pattern**."*

And `cor:attributed` closes the obvious escape — attributes plus arithmetic do not help:

> *"the obstruction is **expressibility, not hardness**: a minimum cut is computable in
> polynomial time, so the quantity is **cheap to compute and impossible to phrase**."*

⭐⭐ The sentence that settles it for this repo, from the same section:

> *"One may ask a biological knowledge graph which proteins are annotated as interacting with a
> given target, and get an answer exactly as good as the curation behind it. One cannot ask
> whether a variant **could have** produced an observed phenotype by an accountable route,
> because that is not a question about what was recorded. **Ontology engineering improves the
> vocabulary in which assertions are made; it does not change what kind of question an assertion
> is able to answer.**"*

### 2.1 What this means for an *exchange*, concretely

Substitute the domain. The retrieval question is *"which consignments are recorded as having come
from this holding"* — answerable exactly as well as whoever entered the records. The admissibility
question is *"could this consignment have come from this holding by an accountable route"* —
and that is the question an exchange actually needs, because it is the one a false record cannot
satisfy by being written down.

⭐ This is the same distinction note 33 already reached from the foreman side, and the user
already ruled on:

> *"If a farmer lies, they will get wrong results and thats it. That does not affect the
> platform. When something is sold, we already have methods to ensure that items are correct
> accordig to our system."*

The papers supply the reason that ruling is safe: the platform's guarantee does not rest on the
record being true, because the platform's question is not a retrieval question. A lie enters as an
assertion, is addressed, and either is or is not accountable against the observations. It never
becomes an edge, because there are no edges to become.

### 2.2 ⚠️ And why a reasoner has nothing to reason over

`thm:query-complete`: every admissibility query is a finite composition of the four operations —
**Identify / Similar / Predict / React**. No fifth operation is required. `cor:query-cost` puts
it at O(k²) trit operations per pair, *"no index, no adjacency structure and no stored triples."*

⭐ So "without a reasoner" is not a design preference or an ideological position about symbolic
AI. It is that **there is nothing underneath the four operations to be queried**. The paper's
closing sentence is the exact statement:

> *"The remedy is not a richer query language over the same data model. It is a **different data
> model**, in which the four operations **are** the query language and **no data model sits
> underneath them to be queried**."*

⚠️ `rem:query-cost-honest` is the fairness caveat and it should be carried, not dropped: the cost
comparison is *"unfair in both directions"*, because *"one retrieves from a finite curated set,
the other computes over an unbounded generated one."*

## 3. ⭐ "Split agents" — and where it lands against the three exclusions

The third leg. `attention-allocated-retrieval.tex` is about exactly this: it tests theorem T2 of
the split-attention agent framework, in *"a federated retrieval pipeline that must join records
across endpoints **before a reasoner can be invoked over them**."*

### 3.1 The split already exists, unnamed and ungated

| Paper | Here |
|---|---|
| Sources with per-source thresholds e\*ᵢ | `Tier.BASE` / `Tier.SPECIALIST` in `lib/ai/stages.js` |
| A budget to allocate | `TOKEN_CAP` in `lib/ai/pipeline.js` — understand 200, ground 200, check 60, refine 500 |
| Per-page agent instances | `agentFor(agentHref)` in `pages/api/assistant/ask.js` — *"every linked page is an agent instance of the main home page model"* |

⚠️ **What is missing is the gate.** `TOKEN_CAP` is a hand-set constant per stage. The paper says
that is the wrong shape in a specific and measurable way.

### 3.2 ⭐ The results that bear on it

- **`thm:threshold`** — e\* = 2ln2/ln(1/q), and **Y(e\*) = N/4 exactly, for every N**. The
  threshold is stated in units of *result*, so ⭐ *"can be evaluated on results already in hand
  rather than on a model of effort."*
- **`thm:reversal`** — a single crossover at u† = 2log₂(1+√2) = 2.5431…, the same for every N.
  Below it, committing the *entire* budget to one source is optimal **and the optimum is the
  endpoint**. Above it, the symmetric split. ⚠️ `rem:not2estar`: the boundary is **not** 2e\* —
  *"Concavity on the interior region is necessary for the interior optimum, not sufficient for
  it."*
- Magnitude: committing beats the even split by ≥ 17.9% over 0.15–0.90 e\*, worst case a **97%
  relative loss**. ⭐ *"the agents with the least attention — the ones an allocation theory exists
  to help — are the ones an ungated allocator hurts most."*
- ⭐ **`prop:normalised` / `cor:perjoin`** — the optimum equalises uᵢ = eᵢ/e\*ᵢ, **not raw eᵢ**.
  *"**Gating must be per source, in normalised units.**"*
- ⭐ **`def:refuse-allocate`** — the allocator returns *a refusal* carrying offered budget,
  required budget, shortfall, and per-source thresholds.

⚠️ `prop:notsufficient`: B ≥ Σe\*ᵢ is necessary but **not sufficient**. The completed proof gives
the interior solution N₁/4 + N₂/4 = 30 exactly, and funding only the larger source yields
**32.629, or 8.76% more** — because marginal yield *rises* to a peak at e\* and falls thereafter,
so withdrawing a source entirely frees enough budget to push another far up its own curve. Subset
selection is therefore by **enumeration**, not a greedy ratio rule.

### 3.3 ⭐ What this repo already got right by measurement, and what it got wrong by constant

`pipeline.js` carries a long comment on why the stages are sequential rather than concurrent:

> *"Measured, that made things worse: both concurrent calls hit the 120s timeout and the whole
> request failed, where the sequential version had completed in 239s. The reason is that Ollama
> serialises requests against a single loaded model... **Concurrency would be the right tool
> against a server that batches. This one does not.**"*

⭐ That is the *same class of finding* as `thm:reversal`, reached by measurement on this
deployment rather than in closed form: **below a threshold, commit rather than split.** The
comment states the mechanism (no batching) and the paper states the condition (u < u†); they are
not the same theorem, and this note should not pretend they are — but they agree that an
ungated splitter is the failure mode.

⚠️ **What is wrong today is `TOKEN_CAP`.** It is raw effort per stage, hand-tuned, not normalised.
`cor:perjoin` says the quantity that should be equalised is eᵢ/e\*ᵢ, and nothing here computes an
e\*ᵢ for a stage. That is a real gap and §7 lists it.

### 3.4 ⭐ Where split agents land against the three AI exclusions — argued, not asserted

`README.md` excludes AI from **the address path**, from **ranking**, and from **deterministic
synthesis** (*"A synthesized entry must be recomputable from the ledger years later by someone who
does not have the model."*).

A reasoner generating edges would violate the third exclusion outright — a generated edge is a
synthesised relation that a later reader cannot recompute without the model that generated it.
That is not a marginal call.

⭐ **Split agents as described do not, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of
degree:** the edges are **computed**, not inferred. `cor:no-edges` generates an edge by evaluating
`f(ℓ(u,v))` on two addresses. That is arithmetic on `olduvai-core` data with no model in the loop,
so a reader years later recomputes it from the ledger and gets our bytes. The agents allocate
*attention* — which source gets budget — and never produce a relation.

⚠️ **The boundary is therefore exactly where it already is, and one thing must not move across
it.** The allocator decides *what to spend effort on*. It must not decide *what is admissible*.
The moment an agent's allocation decision changes an answer's content rather than its cost, the
allocator has become a ranker, and ranking is excluded. The existing rule in
`lib/api/upstream.js` — a BFF route may only *attach identity, forward, reshape, and fail
honestly* — is the right shape and should be restated for agents when they are built.

### 3.5 ⭐⭐ The refusal principle, which this repo already implements

**`prin:refusal`:**

> *"A representation earns a positive result only if some presentable object would have received
> a negative one. **A framework with no refusals has an empty defined class: it does not classify,
> it merely accepts.**"*

**`thm:refusal-nonvacuous`:**

> *"A gate that never closes is not a gate; a gate that always closes is not one either; and **a
> refusal that misreports its own remedy is worse than no refusal, because it sends the caller to
> acquire a budget that will be declined again.**"*

⭐ The twelve-entry `GATES` table in `navigation.js` is `prin:refusal` implemented. Its header
already says so in its own words — *"'Coming soon' would be a smaller lie than fake data but a
lie all the same"* — and `no-forecast-method` is a gate that says outright it may never lift.

⭐⭐ And the third clause of `thm:refusal-nonvacuous` is the defect fixed in the previous session,
found by a live run and not by reading the paper: `blockedBy: "no-model"` was being returned for a
stage that *timed out on an installed, answering model*, telling someone whose machine is slow to
`ollama pull` a model they already had. That is precisely *"a refusal that misreports its own
remedy... sends the caller to acquire a budget that will be declined again."* The fix —
`model-too-slow` as a separate gate across `pipeline.js`, `ask.js` and `navigation.js` — was
arrived at independently and the paper names it. Recording the convergence is worth more than
either fact alone: it is evidence the gate discipline here is not stylistic.

⚠️ **The one thing to import that is not yet here:** `def:refuse-allocate` carries *offered
budget, required budget, shortfall, and per-source thresholds*. Our gates carry `title`, `detail`,
`reference` — a reason but no numbers. A gate that said *"this needs 4× the tokens the cap
allows"* is a strictly better gate than one that says *"the model is too slow"*, and it is
computable.

## 4. ⭐ What `decision-resolution.tex` contributes, which is a discipline rather than a feature

This is the paper that does not fit the causal-graph story and is the more immediately useful of
the three, because every result in it applies to code that exists.

### 4.1 ⭐⭐ `ex:plate` is note 35 §5.1's open problem, stated exactly

Note 35 §5.1 ended on an unresolved sentence: *"nothing makes the trie depth agree with the
metres."* The paper has the sentence in its own domain. A plate reader on [0,4] to three decimals
gives N = 4001 and β̄ ≈ 10⁻³ OD, and therefore:

> *"**Any ranking of variants whose OD differences are below 10⁻³ is a ranking of quantiser
> codes, not of variants.**"*

⭐ Substitute: **any ranking of holdings whose address differences are below the observation
resolution is a ranking of trie codes, not of holdings.** That is the failure mode `footprint.rs`
exists to prevent, and it is now stateable as a number rather than as a caution.

### 4.2 ⭐ And `prop:dimensional` says what *kind* of problem it is

The paper catches itself building an invalid composite chain β ≤ w_cell ≤ 1/(2B_proc) and rejects
it: *"is not well formed"*, because its left member is a length in state space and its right a
time. `prop:two-constraints` replaces it with three separate well-formed constraints —

1. temporal: h ≤ 1/(2B)
2. spatial: w_cell ≥ hL
3. floor: w_cell ≥ β_inst = D/N

— and a feasibility triangle max{β_inst, hL} ≤ w_cell with h ≤ 1/(2B).

⭐ **Note 35 §5.1 is a units problem of exactly this species**, and the paper supplies the correct
decomposition rather than a warning. `constrains: "within"` from a 30 m DEM cell and `within` from
a 90 m one are different observations — `observe/[source].js` already says so in prose — and the
fix is not to make trie depth "mean metres" but to write down the separate constraints and check
the triangle. ⭐ `rem:commissioning`: this is checkable **before a system is built**, and **can
come back infeasible**, in which case *"the remedy is a faster or finer instrument, not a finer
grid."*

### 4.3 ⚠️⚠️ The floor is two different floors and they must not be merged

This is the most important warning in this note, because both papers use the word.

- **`def:beta-inst`** — the **instrumental** floor D/N, *"available from any instrument's
  specification"*.
- **`def:beta-epi`** — an **epistemic** floor, *"any positive lower bound on distinguishability
  derived from a claim about the observer's relation to what it observes — for instance, that the
  observed environment cannot be exhaustively enumerated by a bounded observer."*

⚠️ **`causal-knowledge-graphs.tex`'s floor β := min ρ(v) > 0 is derived from its Non-completability
axiom — it is a `β_epi`.** `decision-resolution.tex` explicitly warns against merging the two:

> *"Every theorem in this paper uses only β_inst... A reader who accepts only [def:beta-inst]
> loses nothing in what follows. We consider this separation a substantive contribution rather
> than bookkeeping, because the two claims have very different evidential status and **conflating
> them makes the weaker one load-bearing for the stronger one's consequences**."*

⭐ For this repo: a resolution claim about a DEM tile or a GPS fix is `β_inst` and is on a
specification sheet. A claim about addresses never collapsing to a point is `β_epi` and rests on
an axiom. **Any page or gate that quotes a number must say which it is**, and the two must never
be added, compared, or substituted.

### 4.4 ⭐ `thm:occupied-cell` is the right form of the floor for `footprint.rs`

⚠️ `prop:infimum-false` first: the customary argument — *"let β = min diam(C); by finiteness
β > 0"* — is **false**. The sign-with-zero quantiser on [−1,1] has cells [−1,0), {0}, (0,1] and
minimum diameter 0, and it is *"not pathological; it is an exact-zero detector, a standard
component."* ⭐ *"pigeonhole does give a positive bound — but on the **maximum** cell, not the
minimum. **Passing from 'some cell is large' to 'every cell is large' is where the argument fails,
and the two statements have opposite quantifier structure.**"*

**`thm:occupied-cell`** is the repair: P[diam(cell(q)) ≥ D/(2N)] ≥ 1 − MD/2, and ≥ ½ for uniform
μ. ⭐ *"**This is the precise sense in which the floor is real**: not that every cell is wide, but
that **the cells one actually lands in are wide with high probability**."*

⭐ For `footprint::Reading::is_distinguishing_for`: the right question is not *"what is the
smallest cell in the trie"* but *"how wide is the cell this holding actually lands in"*. Those
differ, and the first is the one that is easy to compute and wrong.

### 4.5 ⭐ `thm:saturation` + `rem:placement` — how deep to address, and when to stop

**`thm:saturation`**: Gap\*(N) = 0 for all N ≥ N₀ once the optimal partition is realisable — past
that, further resolution buys nothing.

⭐ **`rem:placement`**: with a threshold at an irrational point and cells constrained uniform,
Gap ≥ ½ **however large N becomes**. *"**Resolution does not repair misplacement**"* — verified
numerically to N = 128. And saturation is **not monotone in N**: adding a cell can destroy an
alignment a coarser monitor possessed.

⭐ `rem:designer` from §6 gives the purchasing rule directly: each additional bit divides the
worst-case decision loss by 2^p — four for a quadratic objective, two for Lipschitz — *"once N
reaches the number of distinct optimal actions further bits buy nothing: **the correct advice is
then to reposition cells, not to add them**."*

That is a direct answer to "how deep should the trie address go", and the answer is not "as deep
as possible". ⚠️ It also means a deeper address can be *worse*, which is not intuitive and should
be written into `trie.rs` when the depth is chosen.

⚠️ `rem:curse`: in d dimensions diam(C) ≥ 2(λ(Q)/(N ω_d))^{1/d}. *"Halving the linear resolution
of a d-dimensional monitor costs a factor 2^d in output states."* At d = 5 a 10⁶-state monitor
achieves coarser linear resolution than a 10²-state monitor at d = 1. Relevant to `fusion.rs`,
which folds four observation sources.

### 4.6 ⭐⭐ `thm:byzantine` — four sources, not three, and the user's own ruling is why it matters

`prop:crash`: three channels suffice against a channel that **dies**. `prop:naive-liar`: three
**still** suffice against a liar reporting *independently* of the honest channels.
**`thm:byzantine`**: against an **adaptive** fault, n ≥ 3f+1 = 4 is necessary and sufficient for
f = 1 — the faulty channel observes an honest reading and reports a value within tolerance of it
but displaced from the truth, *"manufacturing a cluster of two"*, and the configuration is
indistinguishable from one where the dissenter is faulty.

⭐⭐ **`rem:triplicate`** is the sentence that lands on this repo:

> *"Triplicate protects against an instrument that **dies** and against one that fails
> **obviously**. It does not protect against an instrument whose drift produces readings
> resembling genuine ones, because **a miscalibrated instrument is precisely an adaptive fault**:
> its output tracks the true signal with an offset and so clusters with an honest channel.
> Robustness against one miscalibrated instrument requires four."*

⭐ **The exchange has exactly four position sources** — GPS, flights, terrain, satellites — and
the user's own ruling explains why that is the right number rather than a coincidence:

> *"Aircrafts are not a positioning check, but another gps instrument. We do not check any gps
> against another gps... the fact that the aeroplane has flown above a point, means that, we now
> have a gps track in that direction, thats all."*

⚠️ Four *sources* is not yet four *independent* channels, and the theorem is about independence.
A `Corridor` from a flight and a `Fix` from a handset may share an underlying GNSS constellation,
in which case a constellation-level bias is a single adaptive fault seen four times. That is an
open question, not a claim, and it belongs in §7.

⚠️ Figure 5 of the paper records an honest gap: empirically the adaptive liar *"is resolved only
from n = 5 under this majority rule"* — 4 suffices only with the Oral Messages algorithm. So "four
is enough" is enough *with the right combining rule*, and `fusion.rs`'s rule is a Kalman fold,
which is neither.

### 4.7 ⭐ `thm:causal-bias` — the exchange changes what it then observes

**`thm:causal-bias`**: if an estimand is affected by the control policy, bias → E[θ|E] − E[θ] ≠ 0
almost surely, *"bounded away from zero uniformly in n and not reduced by collecting more
samples"* — increasing n drives the estimate **toward** the biased value.

⭐ An exchange that matches participants and then observes what they do is exactly a
policy-coupled estimator. Reference prices, coalition composition, corridor traffic: all of these
are affected by the matching, and then read back as if they were exogenous.

⭐ **`rem:architectural`** gives the only remedies: *"either a held-out channel sampled
independently of the policy, or an explicitly reweighted estimator... Regulators are exempt
because they estimate nothing."*

⭐ **`cor:bias-detect`** is the one to write on a gate: *"A monitor with instrumental floor β_inst
cannot detect a causal bias of magnitude below β_inst, since the biased and unbiased estimates
then fall in a common cell."* A bias smaller than the resolution is invisible, and no amount of
data reveals it.

⚠️ This bears directly on `no-forecast-method`, the gate that *"may never lift"*. It is now a
sharper argument than the one written there: a forecast built on exchange-observed quantities is
policy-coupled by construction, so it is biased by `thm:causal-bias` and the bias does not shrink
with more seasons. The gate's existing wording — *"a plausible unrecomputable number is worse than
no number"* — is right for a different reason than the one it gives, and both reasons should be in
it.

## 5. ⚠️ What did not survive, and what is not established

Following notes 34 and 35: the part worth writing down is what is being declined.

**⚠️ The ontology layer, entirely.** Not as a simplification — `thm:query-separation` is the
argument, and `cor:crossref` plus the representation-mobility remark are the reinforcement:
reconciling representations under this scheme is *"a change of coordinates, and it is
computable... in sharp contrast to reconciling two ontologies' term assignments, which... is not
computable at all."* Nothing is gained by adding a term vocabulary and something is lost.

**⚠️ The reasoner, and it is not a rejection of reasoning.** §2.2: there is nothing underneath the
four operations for a reasoner to operate on. A reasoner added here would have to first construct
the stored graph the whole design avoids.

**⚠️ The S-entropy coordinate construction, as-is.** The paper's ς is built from three physical
observables (K, T, E) that are *pairwise independent and jointly sufficient* for amino acids, with
a measured minimum separation d_min = 0.0756 (Lys–Arg) giving a guaranteed depth k ≥ 9 and an
empirical k = 7. ⭐ **We have no such triple for consignments and no measured d_min.** Adopting
the machinery without the observables would be adopting the notation. ⚠️ `rem:guarantee-slack`
even warns about mistaking the guaranteed depth for the required one — *"a practitioner who reads
the guaranteed depth as the required depth pays that factor for nothing"*.

**⚠️ `thm:weighting-derived` — derived, and its derivation does not transfer for free.**
`w = Λ/‖ς(u)−ς(v)‖` follows from positivity, invariance and **isotropy** (`prop:wt-constraints`),
and ⭐ `rem:reciprocal` — *"**The reciprocal is what makes 'contact' and 'similarity' the same
measurement**"*. ⚠️ `rem:isotropy` says the response to anisotropic coordinates is *"to rescale
the coordinates before encoding, not to break isotropy afterwards"*. Note 34 §9 already lists
anisotropic covariance for `Corridor` as open. **These are the same open question**, and the paper
says which way to resolve it.

**⚠️ Trie-factoring is the single empirical hypothesis, and it has known exceptions.** §16: in the
protein case the exact-computation fraction falls 1.00 → 0.23 with disulfides, covalent
constraints and metal coordination. ⭐ `rem:complexity-honest` says the degradation is graceful —
*"the trie chain still furnishes an upper bound on σ(v), and the gap is bounded by the total
weight of the non-factoring edges"* — but it is a hypothesis, and here it is untested. **⭐ This is
what the cohesion gate already is.** `GATES["cohesion-gate"]`: *"if the intrinsic triple does not
cluster, everything built on it is built on sand. That test has not been run, and it is allowed to
fail."* The papers do not lift that gate. They tell us what it is a test *of*.

⚠️ And the honest limit the paper states about itself, which applies with more force here:

> *"The empty dictionary does not make identification **free** — it makes it **unstored**, which

> of the empirical record."*

## 6. ⭐ Methodological residue — the part that changes how things get tested here

Three results are about *validation*, and they bear on the cohesion test, which has not been run
and is allowed to fail.

**⭐⭐ `rem:selfconsistency`:**

> *"The broken allocator satisfied its own optimality condition exactly: marginals were equalised
> to 10⁻¹⁰. A test asserting 'the returned allocation equalises marginal yield' would have passed.
> What caught it was checking against exhaustive search over the simplex — a procedure sharing no
> line of reasoning with the allocator. This generalises: **an optimality check is only
> informative if it is not the optimality condition the implementation was built from.**"*

⭐ Note 35 §4.2's `doctor` agreement check — *"whether the CLI and the server compute the same
bytes for a known input"* — is exactly such a check, and this is the argument for why it should
not be deferred. `captured()` in `ask.js` is the sibling move on the failure-shape side: it avoids
two hand-maintained copies rather than testing one against the other.

**⭐ "Figures compute their own reference lines."** The paper's `thm:reversal` originally asserted
a crossover at 2e\* and was caught by its own figure, which *computes* the crossover (2.5431)
rather than drawing the predicted value. *"the proof passed inspection because every individual
step in it is true — it simply never compared the interior stationary point against the
endpoints."* Any figure this repo ships about resolution or matching should compute its own
reference line.

**⭐ "A check that cannot discriminate between the hypothesis and its competitor is not evidence
for it, whatever its verdict says."** The paper found a check that passed *vacuously* — corpora of
12 and 300 where the optimum happens to coincide with the commit policy — and replaced it with a
sweep at budget 327.13 where the optimum is strictly interior and strictly beats both naive
policies. ⚠️ **The cohesion test must be designed so it can fail**, and specifically so that
passing distinguishes clustering from an artefact of the coordinate choice.

⭐ A fourth, smaller: the paper runs a **positive control on the detector** — coupon-collector
retrieval is analytically concave, so the diminishing-returns detector is run against it *first*,
since *"A failure there would indicate a broken detector rather than a finding about the world."*
Same shape as `probe()` in `lib/ai/ollama.js` establishing the model is answering before any stage
is blamed.

⚠️ And one to carry as a caution rather than a method: `decision-resolution`'s **check B1c**,
where three apparent violations at coarse resolution **inverted** under refinement, moving from
−93%, −33%, −29% relative slack to +92%, +94%, +94%. *"These were discretisation artefacts of the
estimator, not counterexamples."* A failing cohesion test at coarse resolution is not yet a
failing cohesion test.

⭐ Both papers report having been **refuted by their own suites** — `decision-resolution` records
that *"the numerical suite refuted an earlier global form of the main theorem and forced the
in-cell statement, rather than presenting the corrected theorem as though it had been the
original"*, and `attention-allocated-retrieval` records four errors including two of its own. That
is the standard this repo's notes have been holding, and it is worth naming that the sources hold
it too.

## 7. What this implies, in order

Nothing below is started.

1. **⭐ Reconcile trie depth with observation resolution, using `prop:two-constraints`.** This
   was already item 5 of note 35 and it moves to first because the papers turn it from a
   vocabulary problem into three writeable constraints with a feasibility check that can come back
   infeasible (§4.2). It also gates most of what follows: `ex:plate` says a ranking below the floor
   is a ranking of codes (§4.1).
2. **⭐ Distinguish `β_inst` from `β_epi` wherever a floor is quoted** (§4.3). Cheap, purely
   documentary, and prevents the specific error the source paper calls out — making the weaker
   claim load-bearing for the stronger one's consequences.
3. **⭐ Restate `footprint::Reading::is_distinguishing_for` in `thm:occupied-cell`'s form** (§4.4)
   — the cell a holding *lands in*, not the smallest cell in the partition. ⚠️ Includes checking
   whether the current test is the `prop:infimum-false` argument in disguise.
4. **⭐ Give gates their numbers, per `def:refuse-allocate`** (§3.5) — offered, required,
   shortfall, per-source thresholds. The smallest visible improvement here and it applies to the
   twelve gates that already exist.
5. **⭐ Normalise the stage budget** (§3.3) — `TOKEN_CAP` is raw effort; `cor:perjoin` says the
   equalised quantity is eᵢ/e\*ᵢ. Requires measuring an e\* per stage first, which is a
   measurement this deployment can actually make.
6. **⭐ Write the causal reading of the graph down where `/dashboard/graph` says it** (§1) — the
   blurb is already correct and the page has no text explaining why there is no edge table.
   ⚠️ Behind the cohesion gate, and it stays there.
7. **⭐ Determine whether the four position sources are four independent channels** (§4.6). Open
   question, and the answer changes whether `thm:byzantine`'s n = 4 is satisfied or whether we
   have one channel counted four times. ⚠️ Also: `fusion.rs`'s Kalman fold is not the combining
   rule the theorem's n = 4 assumes.
8. **⭐ Add the policy-coupling argument to `no-forecast-method`** (§4.7) — `thm:causal-bias` is a
   sharper reason than the one currently written, and `cor:bias-detect` says a bias below the
   floor is undetectable regardless of how many seasons are collected.

⚠️ **Not on the list, deliberately:**

- **Any ontology, term vocabulary, or reasoner** (§2, §5). Declined on `thm:query-separation`,
  not on effort.
- **Any stored edge table, adjacency structure, or `causes`-labelled relation** (§1.1). The design
  already has none and the papers say why keeping it that way is the whole point.
- **Porting the S-entropy coordinate construction** (§5) — we have no measured observable triple
  for consignments and no d_min, and adopting the machinery without them adopts the notation only.
- **Anything that lets an allocator decide admissibility** (§3.4). Allocation decides cost.
  Admissibility is computed. The moment those merge, the allocator is a ranker and ranking is
  excluded.
- **Lifting the cohesion gate** (§5). The papers describe what the gate tests. They do not
  substitute for running it, and it is still allowed to fail.
